MESOLITHIC AGES

 In the Paleolithic era, man was a food gatherer and had not yet known stability. The industry of this era includes a group of distinctive stone tools, including bifacial tools, scrapers of various types, knives and pointed tips that were found in many locations. Among the archaeological sites that contained archaeological tools dating back to the Paleolithic era, or the Lower, we find:
1

MESOLITHIC AGES

 Asila Archaeological Site: Located near Umm Bab, about 5 km west of Al-Kharsa'ah, the Danish and French missions worked there, and traces of prehistoric man were discovered there, as a group of stone workshops were found at the site that were dedicated to making weapons and tools that were used -mostly- for hunting. In addition to finding many flint tools, and several thousand stone fragments. In addition, a shattered residential floor and many stone fragments were found in a high area south of Dukhan. The archaeological finds are concentrated on the surface and in the middle of the plateau. Many unskilled flint stones were also found. In another area near Dukhan, a site was also found with a large amount of unworked flint stones, and there is a place that was dedicated to cutting stones, with small blades and fragments scattered on it.

 Two sites were also found about ten kilometers north of Dukhan near Ras Uwaynat Ali. It is noted that the relatively high rock layers at that place protect the open coast from the south, thus providing an ideal location for a camp for fishermen and divers, as is the case in many parts of the world. With the exception of some windblown dunes, the ground surface in that location does not rise more than two meters above sea level at high tide. Among those dunes, we find thousands of flakes and rock fragments left over from flint tool manufacturing operations in two locations separated by a distance of approximately two hundred meters. The flint material we found is similar to that found in Bahrain and dates back to the same period - the Paleolithic era - the colors of the flint range between dark and light brown

 With the advent of agriculture, Neolithic humans switched to settling down in permanent places. Al Jubeigib site, southeast of the city of Dukhan, as well as the site of the city of Zarqa are the Neolithic sites in Qatar. The south of Qatar includes a number of important sites where archeological finds dating back to prehistoric times were found, such as the Zarqa site, where several expeditions embarked on excavation missions, such as the Danish expedition in 1961, the British expedition in 1973 and the French expedition in 1984. These expeditions unearthed some rare potsherds."

We only found one sample that resembled an axe in its size and formulation, but it had one edge. We also find the Khor burial mounds, which the Danish mission attributed to the middle of the Paleolithic, and in which flint tools, awls and fragments with a pointed tip were found, in addition to some cylindrical and transparent blocks and chisels.

 الصخور المتبقية عن عمليات صناعة الأدوات الصوانية في موقعين تفصلهما مسافة مئتي متر تقريبا ومادة الصناعة من الصوان التي عثرنا عليها تشبه تلك التي عثر عليها في البحرين وترجع إلى نفس الفترة – العصر الحجري القديم - تتراوح ألوان الصوان بين البني الغامق والفاتح. كما عثر أيضا على عدد من الرقائق والشظايا، أما النصال فلم نجد منها شيئاً، ولعل أكثر ما وجدناه من الأدوات نوع من الرقائق السميكة ذات الحواف المسننة كالمنشار، ولم نعثر إلا على عينة واحدة تشبه الفأس في حجمها وصياغتها، ولكنها ذات حد واحد. كما نجد تلال الخور المدفنية، التي نسبته البعثة الدانماركية إلى أواسط العصر الحجري القديم، وتم العثور فيه على الأدوات الصوانية، والمخارز والشظايا ذات السن المدبب، بالإضافة إلى بعض الكتل الأسطوانية والشفافة والأزامل.
